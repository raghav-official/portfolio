Attribution & customer journey analyticsSimulated data

Marketing Journey Intelligence

An end-to-end attribution project (Excel, SQL Server, Power BI) that compares four attribution models, classifies each channel by its role in the funnel, and finds the journey sequences that convert best.

It was built to show how the choice of attribution model changes which channels look valuable.

  • 5,000journeys
  • 18,620touchpoints
  • 4attribution models
  • Excel
  • SQL Server Express
  • SSMS
  • T-SQL
  • Power BI
  • DAX
  • Python
GitHub repository
The problem

Last-click credit rewards the channel that closes a sale and ignores the ones that start and shape the journey. The project asks which channels actually influence customers across the whole journey.

Objectives
  • Compare four attribution models on one dataset
  • Classify each channel by its funnel role
  • Identify the journey sequences that convert best
  • Validate the numbers before building the dashboard
  • Document the assumptions and limitations
Approach
  1. 01

    Generate the data

    A Python script produces a synthetic B2B SaaS dataset for a fictional company: 5,000 journeys and 18,620 touchpoints, with channels weighted toward funnel stages so the models disagree.

  2. 02

    Check in Excel

    The touchpoint data is reviewed and quality-checked in Excel.

  3. 03

    Load into SQL Server

    The touchpoints go into a SQL Server table in a database named CobaltMetrics.

  4. 04

    Build the views

    T-SQL views (CTEs, window functions, STRING_AGG) prepare the journey and attribution data.

  5. 05

    Validate

    A validation script confirms 18,620 rows, 5,000 journeys and 928 conversions, with 928 attributed conversions under every model.

  6. 06

    Build the dashboard

    Power BI loads the table and views, and DAX measures drive four pages: Journey Overview,Attribution Comparison, Journey Intelligence and Decision Center.

Key analysis
  • Four-model attribution comparison, including First Touch and Last Touch
  • Funnel-role classification: Awareness, Intent and Close
  • Journey sequence analysis by opening channel
  • Journey length and conversion by number of touchpoints
  • A Decision Center page with a recommendation pattern for each channel
Key findings
  • Model choice changes channel value. Email gets 39.7% of conversions under Last Touch but 4.6% under First Touch. Google Ads is the reverse: 20.5% and 7.8%.
  • Early-funnel channels are invisible to last-click. LinkedIn Ads, Meta Ads, Content Marketing and Referral get 0% Last Touch, yet LinkedIn is the first touch for 18.6% of converted journeys and Content for 11.7%.
  • Channels play distinct roles. Meta and Referral work mainly at Awareness, Content, Retargeting and Webinar at Intent, and Email and Direct close.
  • An opening channel followed by Email converts best: Meta Ads to Email 41.7%, Referral to Email 38.5%, Google Ads to Email 36.0%, against 18.6% overall. These rest on 5 to 10 conversions each, so they are directional.
  • Most journeys never convert: 4,072 of 5,000 (81.4%) are lost. The 928 conversions average 3.72 touchpoints and 13.68 days, and Considered Buyers (3 to 5 touchpoints) are the largest converting group.

All figures come from synthetic data. They show the method, not real market results.

What it demonstrates

The project shows how I approach attribution: question the default model, compare alternatives, validate the numbers and state the limits. Its recommendation pattern is to protect Email as the main closer, invest in LinkedIn and Content because they start journeys, optimise Google Ads after checking cost per acquisition, and investigate Meta Ads traffic that does not reach intent.

Limits and next steps

There is no channel spend, so cost per acquisition is not measured. Possible extensions: add spend, analyse at account level, and test a data-driven (Markov) attribution model.

Multi-Channel Marketing Performance & Budget Optimisation

An end-to-end project that simulates two years of performance across six acquisition channels and analyses it through an Excel, SQL Server and Power BI pipeline, ending in a five-page dashboard with a live what-if budget simulator.

It was built to answer practical budget questions: how channels perform, where customers drop out, and how a fixed monthly budget could be split.

  • 2,184Campaign Records
  • 6channels
  • 24months
  • Excel
  • SQL Server
  • Power BI
  • DAX
GitHub repository
The problem

Spend is spread across channels with very different costs and roles, and last-click numbers can make some of them look weak. The project asks which channels perform, which are profitable, and where a fixed budget should go.

Objectives
  • Assess how each channel performs and brings in customers
  • Find where customers drop out of the funnel
  • Identify which channels are profitable
  • Decide how to distribute ₹50L for the next month
  • Test what happens when the budget goes up or down
Approach
  1. 01

    Simulate the dataset

    Two years of weekly data (Jan 2024 to Dec 2025): 6 channels with 3 to 4 named campaigns each, 2,184 rows, including festive-season spikes and a monsoon slowdown. Assumption ranges are documented in the Channel Benchmarks sheet.

  2. 02

    Track the full funnel

    Each row follows Impressions, Clicks, Leads, MQLs, SQLs, Customers and Revenue.

  3. 03

    Prepare in Excel

    The workbook holds the raw data, a data dictionary, channel benchmarks and the budget optimisation model.

  4. 04

    Query in SQL Server

    A relational schema, views and queries answer the business questions, with the CSV imported as a staging step.

  5. 05

    Build the dashboard

    Power BI has five pages: Marketing Overview, Funnel Analysis, Channel Analysis, Budget Optimization and a What-If Budget Simulator, with DAX measures.

  6. 06

    Reallocate the budget

    The same ₹50L monthly budget is redistributed using a response-curve-optimised mix and compared with the current split.

Key analysis
  • Channel performance: ROAS and customer acquisition cost (CAC)
  • Full-funnel conversion from impressions to revenue
  • Profitability comparison across the six channels
  • Budget optimisation using a response-curve model
  • A live what-if simulator for raising or cutting spend
Key findings
  • Blended ROAS across all channels is 2.15x.
  • Email is the standout at 23.8x ROAS: low cost and a high-intent, existing audience.
  • Google Ads is the solid workhorse at 1.8x ROAS and the largest scalable paid channel.
  • Meta and YouTube are below 1x on last-click ROAS, which likely reflects a stronger upper-funnel role than direct response.
  • LinkedIn has the highest CAC (about ₹18,000) and the highest average order value, which suits enterprise deals more than pure efficiency.
  • Reallocating the same ₹50L monthly budget to the optimised mix projects about a 51% revenue improvement.

The data is simulated and non-confidential. The 51% figure is a model projection on simulated data, not a business result.

What it demonstrates

The project shows how I connect funnel data to spend: compare channels on ROAS and CAC, read results in context, and turn the analysis into a budget recommendation with a scenario tool that people can explore.
